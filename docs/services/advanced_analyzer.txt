# Multi-Detector Spectral Analysis for SPHEREx

## What It Is About  
SPHEREx carries three physically separated telescopes, each with two spectral detectors sensitive to different infrared wavelength ranges:

- Telescope 1: Detectors D1 (0.75–1.09 μm) and D4 (2.42–3.82 μm)  
- Telescope 2: Detectors D2 (1.10–1.62 μm) and D5 (3.83–4.41 μm)  
- Telescope 3: Detectors D3 (1.63–2.41 μm) and D6 (4.42–5.00 μm)

Any object in the sky is observed by only one telescope per visit, providing spectral information at two wavelengths (one per detector in the pair).

---

## How It Works (Step-by-Step)

1. **Determine Detector Pair Covering the Object**  
   For the target celestial coordinates (RA, Dec), the system identifies which telescope pair observes that position.

2. **Aperture Photometry on Both Detectors**  
   For both detectors of the pair, flux (brightness) is measured by summing the light in a small aperture around the object’s pixel position.

3. **Calculate Flux Ratio**  
   The ratio of the long wavelength detector flux to the short wavelength flux is computed, with error propagation to estimate uncertainty.

4. **Classify Source Based on Flux Ratio**  
   - Ratio < 0.7: Strong absorption (e.g., presence of ice for pair D1+D4)  
   - Ratio < 0.9: Weak absorption  
   - Ratio 0.9–1.1: Continuum (likely a star)  
   - Ratio > 1.1: Emission (e.g., warm dust)  
   - Signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) below 3: Classification is undetermined

---

## Detailed Explanation of the Code

- **Blackbody Ratio Calculation (`_bb_fnu_ratio`)**  
  Models the expected flux ratio for a perfect thermal emitter at temperature T, aiding in interpretation.

- **Color Temperature Inversion (`_color_temperature`)**  
  From the measured ratio, calculates an effective “color temperature” assuming blackbody radiation.

- **Spatial Coordinate Conversion (`_pixel_for_world`)**  
  Converts sky coordinates (RA/Dec) to pixel coordinates on the detector using spatial WCS transformations.

- **Detector Photometry (`_photometry_detector`)**  
  For a detector, extracts the flux, error, SNR, and pixel position corresponding to the celestial point.

- **Flux Ratio and Classification Computation**  
  Calculates ratio and its error, applying cuts based on flux validity and SNR, then classifies the source based on temperature ranges reflecting stellar or dust properties.

---

## Public Interface: `analyze_pair`

Given sector, epoch, and sky coordinates, this function:

- Finds the telescope detector pair covering the point.  
- Performs photometry to obtain fluxes and SNRs.  
- Computes flux ratio and error.  
- Classifies the object (hot star, sun-like star, cool star, dusty object, or undetermined).  
- Returns detailed results for the short and long detectors, ratio, and classification.

If no detector pair covers the location, it returns an undetermined classification.

---

## Why It Matters

- Enables physical interpretation of sources by comparing flux at two strategically separated infrared bands.  
- Helps identify features like ice absorption, dust emission, or stellar types from minimal spectral points.  
- Supports efficient spectral classification without full spectroscopy, optimizing SPHEREx’s survey data utility.
